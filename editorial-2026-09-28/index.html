Who stays,
who leaves?

Following participant attrition
through randomized depression trials.

Randomized trials compare assigned groups. But participants do not always remain through completion. A trial’s overall dropout rate can hide whether more people leave one arm than the other.

01 / The overall picture

How much attrition
is there?

Across 82 usable trials, the median trial reports 13.66% non-completion. Yet that typical value sits inside a much wider distribution.

Each dot is a trial. The shaded band contains the middle half; vertical displacement only separates dots. The 100% record reports zero completions and remains flagged—not accepted as verified mass withdrawal. Click or focus a dot for its counts.

Counting participants instead of giving each trial equal weight: 3,275 of 20,949 did not complete (15.63%). Both summaries use STARTED, not enrollment.

But a trial-level rate cannot tell us whether its randomized arms lost participants equally.

02 / Inside the same randomized trial

The same trial.
Different rates of leaving.

Do participants leave the two arms equally? In 38 reliably paired trials, the median absolute gap is 3.52 percentage points. Individual signed differences range from −20 to +25 points.

There is no consistent direction: experimental-arm attrition is higher in 14 trials, comparator-arm attrition in 19, and five are equal. The finding is variation in asymmetry—not that one kind of arm always loses more people.

03 / Looking for an explanation

Can trial characteristics
explain the variation?

Our available characteristics do not establish a clear, robust explanation for overall attrition in this extracted sample. A small model of trial size, sponsor class and phase uses 82 trials; all three bootstrap intervals cross zero.

This model concerns the overall trial rate, not the experimental–comparator gap. It cannot tell us why a particular pair of arms diverges.

The sponsor estimate changes substantially when the flagged zero-completion record is omitted. That sensitivity is a reason for caution—not evidence that trial characteristics do not matter.

04 / From participant attrition to information availability

As the question gets narrower,
so does the usable evidence.

We started with people who did not complete a trial. Now the limiting factor is what we can compare in the public record. Each additional question requires more specific, reliably linked information.

    The next step is not yet justified

    A harmonized treatment-effect comparison

    The 14 are candidates for review, not comparable effect estimates. “Change in depression score” can mean different weeks, different people included in the analysis, adjusted or ordinary averages, different handling of missing observations, and different coding directions.

    What we can say

    A single rate
    is only the beginning.

    Attrition varies across these depression trials, and an overall rate can hide substantial randomized-arm differences in some of them. Our limited trial-level analysis offers no clear, robust explanation of overall attrition—and does not explain those arm differences. Investigating the consequences requires outcomes that are comparable, not merely present.

    Following who leaves also means following
    what the public record lets us know.

    Methodology, limitations & reproducibility

    Source and scope

    ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API, retrieved September 13, 2026; source processing September 11. Four immutable local JSON pages, 335 candidate studies, 201 eligible studies. Completed interventional studies with Phase II/III present, randomized parallel design, structured results and explicit major-depression labels; mixed bipolar/schizophrenia labels deferred. This is a selected registry cohort, not a census. Field-level definitions.

    Rates, periods and mappings

    Attrition = 1 − COMPLETED/STARTED, with positive STARTED and valid participant counts; aggregate arm counts before dividing. STARTED is not automatically number randomized. Enrollment differs from STARTED in 11 usable trials and is never substituted. The sole reported period can include follow-up. 51 trials have multiple periods; 106 need protocol/flow arm-mapping review; these categories overlap. No ambiguous period is selected. Exact label matches allow only case/whitespace normalization and must form a bijection.

    Paired contrasts require exactly two groups, one explicitly EXPERIMENTAL and one explicit placebo, active, sham or no-intervention comparator. Registry types—not row order—define roles. NCT00149643 reports zero completions and is retained as a flagged anomaly, with a separate sensitivity analysis. Counts can be arithmetically valid without a resolved interpretation.

    Missingness and selection

    Missing values remain missing. Reasons are not synonym-merged; shares require the complete arm reason sum to reconcile with non-completions. 152 usable arms across 55 trials have reconciled reasons, while 69 otherwise usable arms lack a reason sum. Exact calendar span exists for only 39 usable trials and measures recruitment/study time, not participant follow-up. Sponsor extraction imbalance (52/155 INDUSTRY; 28/43 OTHER) limits generalization.

    Model and outcome limits

    The three-covariate, equal-trial regression estimates associations with overall trial attrition, not differential attrition, causes or predictions. All bootstrap coefficient intervals cross zero; the sponsor estimate is sensitive to the flagged record. HC3 standard errors and diagnostics are exported. We inspected 330 primary outcomes and 1,888 cells. The 14 MADRS-change candidates require adjudication of timepoint, analysis population, adjustment, missing-data handling and direction before an effect analysis. No effect-of-attrition or reporting-bias claim has been established.

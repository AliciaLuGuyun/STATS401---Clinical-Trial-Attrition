01 / The scale of non-completion

A typical rate hides a wide distribution.

Each dot is one trial; horizontal position shows non-completion in its sole reported period. Dot area is constant: large trials do not visually overwhelm small ones. Vertical displacement only separates overlapping dots.

Charts retain readable labels on small screens. Swipe horizontally inside a chart to see its full width.

Median line and interquartile band summarize trials, not participants. Select a dot with a click or Enter. The 100% record reports zero completions; it remains visible as a reporting anomaly, not a verified mass withdrawal.

What does “attrition” mean here?

1 − COMPLETED / STARTED, with STARTED > 0 and 0 ≤ COMPLETED ≤ STARTED. Trial totals sum arm counts first. The sole flow period can include follow-up. Enrollment is a different quantity; STARTED is not automatically the number randomized. Missing values never become zero.

02 / Trial context

Compare distributions before telling a subgroup story.

Study characteristics are available, but some categories contain very few trials. Medians are shown only for groups with at least five observations; this display rule does not remove their individual dots.

Labels show usable / eligible trial counts within the active filters. Differences may reflect selection, interventions, follow-up or sample composition. Sparse categories do not support general conclusions.

Explore continuous characteristics and missingness

Calendar span uses exact start/completion dates only; it includes recruitment and is not participant follow-up duration. Location count is the number of listed registry locations, not verified active sites. No trend or causal model is fitted here.

03 / Within the same randomized trial

Overall retention can conceal arm imbalance.

Each line connects two arms from the same trial. One must be explicitly EXPERIMENTAL and the other an explicit comparator, with an exact one-to-one protocol/flow label match. A second arm is never automatically a control.

● Experimental · ◇ Comparator · Δ = experimental − comparator, percentage points

All eligible pairs in the current filter are shown. Labels and point shapes reinforce color. Differences are descriptive; they are not treatment-effect estimates or significance tests. Click a row to inspect its denominators below.

04 / A test of how far the evidence reaches

Associations are sensitive. Certainty would be premature.

An exploratory equal-trial regression adjusts simultaneously for trial size, industry sponsorship and presence of Phase III. We use continuous attrition in percentage points, avoiding an arbitrary “high attrition” threshold. Three covariates, 82 trials; no automated variable selection.

All displayed 95% trial-bootstrap intervals cross zero. The industry coefficient changes noticeably when one flagged record is removed. These are exploratory associations in a selected registry subset, not causal effects or validated predictions.

Model boundaries

OLS with equal trial weights; log₂(STARTED), industry versus all other sponsor classes, and any Phase III versus no Phase III. Bootstrap resamples whole trials (2,000 draws, seed 401); HC3 standard errors and diagnostics are downloadable. Rates are bounded; linear fits can leave 0–100%, so this model is for association sensitivity, not rate prediction. Sparse intervention categories and inconsistent calendar duration are not entered. Intervals do not account for registry selection, unmeasured confounding or publication/reporting bias.

05 / Why did they leave—or why don’t we know?

Having results is not the same as having comparable results.

All 201 eligible trials report participant flow and outcomes. Our automated extraction can use only a subset. The matrix separates source reporting from analytical eligibility: an unresolved match is not evidence of poor trial conduct.

Filled “✓” = present/passes; open “−” = absent or does not pass that column’s rule. “Reasons” means numeric reason entries exist, not that they explain every non-completion. Outcomes means at least one numeric primary-result cell; it does not mean a comparable treatment effect. All matching trials are shown; scroll for additional rows.

Can we link attrition to treatment effects?

MADRS change, remission and response are different estimands. Even change scores vary by week, direction, adjusted versus unadjusted mean, analysis population and missing-data handling. Outcome groups use their own IDs and must be mapped separately. No pooled effect, significance classification or attrition–efficacy scatterplot is justified yet. What the current data can tell us

The strongest story is variation, within-trial asymmetry, and the boundary between reported and interpretable data. Completeness and selection are part of the evidence—not a footnote. Reasons are useful within reconciled arms; heterogeneous labels and incomplete reporting prevent a universal explanation of why people leave.
